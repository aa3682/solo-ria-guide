# Stay Registered and Compliant

Every step before this one had a beginning, a set of tasks, and a point where you moved on; this one does not work that way. The obligations it covers — keeping the registration form current, renewing the registration itself, delivering documents to existing clients, and keeping records — start once the firm is approved and then repeat every year for as long as it operates. There is no next numbered step waiting on the other side of this one. From here, the firm's day-to-day is governed by the standing subject areas in [Practice Areas](/domains), most heavily Compliance and Supervision and Records and Documentation, rather than by anything with a beginning and an end.

## Why it matters

A registration that was accurate on its effective date does not stay accurate on its own. Clients move on, fees change, an outside activity starts, a disciplinary matter gets resolved — and the file has to keep matching the firm that actually exists. The annual cycle exists because a regulator cannot verify that on its own; it relies on the firm to correct its own record, on a schedule it does not get to opt out of.

Missing this cycle once is rarely dramatic on its own. It produces a slow drift between what the file says and what the firm does instead, the kind that surfaces all at once at examination, when several years of small gaps become one larger finding. The firms that stay clean are not the ones with the fewest obligations; they are the ones that treat these obligations as calendar items due on a schedule, not as decisions to make fresh each time.

## What actually happens

### Keep the registration form current, on the annual cycle and off it

[Write the Disclosure Documents](/process/write-the-disclosure-documents) introduced the annual updating amendment: a yearly filing, timed to the firm's fiscal year end, that confirms or corrects every answer on the firm's registration form. From this point forward, that filing is not a task you complete once — it is the anchor event on a calendar you keep for the rest of the firm's life. Confirm the actual deadline each year on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures) rather than trusting your memory of when it fell last time.

Some changes cannot wait for that date. A set of specific items on the form has to be corrected promptly when it stops being accurate, in what the form's own instructions call an other-than-annual amendment, rather than sitting until the next annual cycle rolls around. Ask, every time something about the firm changes, whether it touches one of those items.

<Callout type="warning">
If you are on the SEC path: which items require a prompt, other-than-annual amendment differs slightly by regulator, since it tracks which parts of the form you file. A state-registered adviser's obligation reaches the state-only portion of the form it alone completes. An SEC-registered adviser's obligation additionally covers Form CRS, the client relationship summary, which has to be amended and redelivered to existing clients whenever anything in it becomes materially inaccurate — an obligation a state-registered adviser, who never prepares Form CRS, does not have.
</Callout>

### Renew the registration itself

Registration has to be renewed, not just described accurately. Once a year, your regulator issues a renewal notice through the same electronic filing system you first used to apply in [File and Get Approved](/process/file-and-get-approved), and the firm pays a renewal fee to keep its registration in effect. Miss the renewal window and the registration can lapse even though nothing else about the firm changed — an entirely avoidable outcome that a standing calendar reminder prevents.

<Callout type="warning">
If you are on the SEC path: renewal does not run through a single home-state fee the way it does for a state-registered adviser. You renew your SEC registration directly, and separately keep current the notice filing and its own fee in every state where you do enough business to require one — the same list built at the first filing.
</Callout>

### Keep meeting delivery obligations to existing clients

The annual brochure delivery obligation described in Write the Disclosure Documents runs every year, not only the year a client first signs on. Each year, deliver the current brochure, or a summary of what changed materially since the last one with an offer to send the full document, and log that delivery the way you logged the first one. Anything material that changes between cycles, particularly a new disciplinary item, goes out to affected clients when it happens rather than waiting for the annual delivery.

### Treat retention as a continuous discipline, not a project

[Write the Policies](/process/write-the-policies) built the recordkeeping scheme: where each category of record lives, in what format, and who can access it. That scheme was designed once. Keeping it accurate is not. Every amendment, delivery, and client communication adds to what the scheme has to capture, and how long each category must be retained is set by rule and lives on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures).

By this point there is no further numbered work waiting for you. What you leave holding instead is a running calendar of amendments, renewals, deliveries, and retained records that repeats every year the firm operates, carried forward through the standing work of the practice areas rather than through any further step in this guide.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Do you have a single calendar, not a mental note, that tracks the annual amendment, the renewal notice, and the annual delivery obligation together?
- Do you know which of your own answers on the registration form would require a prompt, other-than-annual amendment the moment they changed, rather than waiting for the next annual cycle?
- If a client asked for the current brochure today, could you produce it along with a record of when they last received one?
- Do you know how long each category of record you keep must be retained, and could you actually produce it on short notice?
- Now that no further step is forcing a check, who reviews whether the firm's day-to-day practice still matches what its documents say?

## Common mistakes

- Filing the annual updating amendment on time, but missing an item elsewhere on the form that needed a prompt, other-than-annual update the moment it changed.
- Letting the annual brochure delivery slide into a formality, sent late or without a real summary of what changed.
- Assuming a renewal notice is optional or automatic, and discovering the registration lapsed only when a client or the regulator asks about it.
- Building a recordkeeping scheme once at filing and never checking, months or years later, whether new categories of records are actually landing in it.
- Continuing to deliver documents and log activity for a client relationship that has actually ended, instead of closing it out in the records.

## Which practice areas apply

- **Compliance and Supervision**: the annual and other-than-annual amendment cycle, and catching what needs a prompt update, is that area's core work from this point forward.
- **Records and Documentation**: retention, covered here as a continuous discipline rather than a one-time setup, is that area's subject for as long as the firm operates.
- **Firm Economics**: renewal fees and other recurring registration costs are ongoing expenses the firm's economics have to absorb every year, not only at the initial filing.
- **Risk and Insurance**: the coverage and bonding put in place earlier need their own periodic review and renewal, on a rhythm similar to everything else here.
- **Client Education**: explaining what changed in an annual delivery, or an updated relationship summary, in language a client understands, is that area's job every time this cycle repeats.

<Callout type="info">
The single most common way a solo adviser falls out of compliance is not a dramatic failure. It is treating registration as something that happened once, back at approval, rather than as a recurring calendar of obligations that starts there and never stops. Put the annual amendment, the renewal notice, and the annual delivery on the same calendar you use for everything else in the business, and review it on a fixed schedule rather than when something reminds you.
</Callout>
