# Set Up Custody and Technology

Once the firm is filed and approved, it still cannot hold a single client's assets until a specific set of infrastructure exists: a working relationship with a qualified custodian, compliance with the custody rule that governs how client funds and securities are held, and the operational pipes and baseline software that let you actually manage an account day to day. This step covers what has to exist once, before the first client, not how to run or improve any of it afterward.

## Why it matters

Custody sits at the center of what regulators worry about most: an adviser who can move or misappropriate client money without anyone else checking. Nearly every custody rule requirement traces back to that one concern, and nearly every enforcement action in this area starts with a firm that treated a custody detail as a formality instead of a control.

The operational side matters for a plainer reason. A firm that is legally registered but cannot yet see its clients' positions, reconcile them against the custodian's own records, or route a trade cleanly is not actually open for business. Skipping ahead to marketing or client meetings before this step is finished tends to produce a scramble the first time a real account needs something done.

## What actually happens

### Establish the qualified-custodian relationship

Client funds and securities generally have to sit with a qualified custodian: a bank, a registered broker-dealer, or another institution that meets the regulatory definition, rather than in an account you control directly. Selecting one means confirming it can actually hold and trade the account types and instruments your practice plans to offer, then working through its onboarding process to set up the account structure, your trading authority on client accounts, and, if you bill fees by deducting them directly, the mechanics for doing that.

This relationship takes real time to put in place, and it belongs on the critical path well before your first client conversation. An onboarding process that is still open when a client is ready to fund an account turns a firm that is otherwise ready into one that has to ask a new client to wait.

### Meet the custody rule's requirements

"Custody" is a defined term, and it reaches further than most new advisers expect. Holding client assets directly is custody, but so is having the authority to deduct your own fees from a client's account, and in some circumstances so is having a power of attorney broad enough to move money. Whether a given arrangement counts, and what it triggers, depends on the exact rule your state administers.

At a minimum, a state's custody rule generally requires notifying the regulator that you have custody, keeping the assets with a qualified custodian, and making sure the custodian sends account statements directly to clients rather than through you. Advisers whose only form of custody is direct fee deduction can often avoid the full weight of these requirements, including an independent verification of client assets, but only if they meet specific conditions the rule spells out. Most states built their version of this rule around [NASAA's model custody rule](https://www.nasaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/07/IA-Model-Rule-Custody.pdf), though each state can and does adapt it. None of the actual notice deadlines, statement frequencies, or verification triggers belong on this page, since a state can set its own version of each one; see [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures) for what applies where you are registered.

<Callout type="warning">
If you are on the SEC path: [the federal custody rule](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.206(4)-2), not your state's version, governs you, and it is not identical to the state rules it inspired. It sets its own definition of custody, its own account-statement mechanics, and its own independent-verification requirement, with its own exceptions. Confirm which version actually applies to your situation rather than assuming the state rule you researched first carries over.
</Callout>

### Connect the operational pipes

Once the custodial relationship exists, three connections have to work before you can manage an account: a data feed that brings the custodian's record of positions and transactions into your own systems, a reconciliation process that checks your books against the custodian's on a regular schedule, and a way to route trades so an order you place actually reaches the account without being re-keyed by hand at every step.

Test all three before they carry a real client's money. A feed that looks connected in a settings screen and a feed that actually delivers clean, current data are not the same thing, and the gap between them is much cheaper to find during setup than during your first live trade.

### Stand up the baseline systems

Beyond the custodian connections, a handful of systems have to exist before day one: something to track and report on client holdings, something to hold the records the firm is required to keep, and a way to document the compliance work this step and the ones after it produce. None of this needs to be sophisticated at launch. It needs to be in place and actually working.

By the end of this step you hold a firm that can take on a client in practice, not just on paper: a signed custodial relationship, a custody arrangement that satisfies the applicable rule, data and reconciliation pipes that have been tested, and the baseline systems running underneath all of it.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Have you confirmed, specifically, that your chosen custodian can hold and trade every account type and instrument your practice plans to offer?
- Do you know whether your fee-billing method gives you custody under the rule that applies to you, and what that does or does not trigger?
- Has the custodian confirmed how it will send account statements directly to clients?
- Have you tested the data feed and reconciliation process with sample or seed data, rather than waiting for the first live account?
- Do you know exactly how a trade you place reaches the client's account, end to end?
- If a client asked to fund an account tomorrow, is there any piece of this infrastructure that still isn't actually working?

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a custodian before confirming it can serve the specific account types and instruments the practice plans to offer, then discovering the gap after the relationship is already set up.
- Assuming that deducting your own fees doesn't count as custody, and missing the specific conditions required to stay outside the fuller set of requirements.
- Waiting until a real client account is open to test the data feed and reconciliation process for the first time.
- Treating trade routing as something to work out informally once accounts exist, rather than confirming it during setup.
- Starting custodian onboarding late enough that it is still incomplete when the firm's registration becomes effective.

<Callout type="info">
Keep a dated record of every step in the custodian relationship: the account-opening agreements, your trading authority, the fee-deduction authorization if you use one, and the results of your first reconciliation test. If an examiner ever asks how custody actually works at the firm, this is the file that answers the question.
