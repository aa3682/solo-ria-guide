# Choose Your Regulator

Choose Your Regulator determines which regulator your firm registers with: the securities regulator of your home state, or the [Securities and Exchange Commission](/glossary#securities-and-exchange-commission) (SEC). The title is a little misleading, because you do not really choose. The rules decide, based mostly on the size of the assets your firm manages, and this step establishes where your firm falls under them. Your own qualification as an adviser and the filing itself are the next two steps.

## Why it matters

The regulator determines much of what follows: which form you file and where, what fees you pay, which examinations you must pass, and who will one day examine the firm. [Qualify as an Adviser](/process/qualify-as-an-adviser) and [File and Get Approved](/process/file-and-get-approved) both start from the answer, so it has to be settled first.

Almost every solo firm starts state-registered. A new solo practice usually manages less than the SEC registration threshold, and that is the guide's default scenario: a [state-registered adviser](/glossary#state-registered-adviser) whose home state is the regulator. The SEC path exists, and this page marks where it diverges, but for most readers the step means confirming that the default applies rather than choosing between two live options. The determination is also revisited every year, which is part of the final step, [Stay Registered and Compliant](/process/stay-registered-and-compliant).

## What actually happens

### Establish your regulatory assets under management

The dividing line is drawn in [regulatory assets under management](/glossary#regulatory-assets-under-management), often shortened to RAUM. This is a defined term, not a marketing number: it counts the securities portfolios your firm continuously and regularly supervises or manages, calculated the way the [Form ADV General Instructions](https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/formadv-instructions.pdf) describe. It can differ from the figure you would use to describe your book, because the definition is not intuitive.

For a firm that does not exist yet, the figure is a projection of the assets you reasonably expect to manage once clients have moved, using the same definition. Be honest about it in both directions, because it determines the regulator and is reported on the form itself.

### Find where that figure falls

Once you have the figure, compare it to the lines the rules draw. There is a level below which an adviser registers with its home state, a level at or above which it registers with the SEC, and a band in between where the answer depends on the home state. There is also a [buffer on either side of the line](/glossary#buffer-on-either-side-of-the-sec-line), so a registered firm whose assets drift modestly across it does not have to switch right away. None of those figures are printed here, because they are set by statute and rule and can change; each appears by name on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures).

The middle band is where the home state matters most. An adviser in that band generally registers with its state, unless the state does not require it to register or does not examine advisers, in which case the rules send it to the SEC instead. Which category your state falls into is a fact to confirm, not assume. The statute that draws these lines is [section 203A of the Investment Advisers Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title15-section80b-3a&num=0&edition=prelim).

<Callout type="warning">
If you are on the SEC path: your figure sits at or above the SEC level, or inside the middle band in a state that sends you to the SEC. Your home state no longer registers the firm; you file with the SEC and make [notice filings](/glossary#notice-filing) in the states whose rules require them.
</Callout>

### Check whether an exception applies

The asset figure is the main test, but not the only one. Section 203A of the statute and [SEC Rule 203A-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.203A-2) together carve out several categories of adviser that may or must register with the SEC regardless of assets, and the exact categories live there, not here. The one most likely to touch a solo firm is the rule's exception for an adviser that would otherwise have to register in many states at once. Others, like the statute's treatment of an adviser to a registered fund, are situations a new solo practice rarely faces.

Most exceptions have a specific count or figure attached, and those belong on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures), not here. What belongs here is the habit of checking: before concluding that the default applies, read the list and confirm none of it describes your firm.

<Callout type="warning">
If you are on the SEC path: an exception, rather than your assets, may be what put you there, and it has to keep being true. If the exception stops applying, you switch back to state registration on the timeline the SEC's rules set.
</Callout>

### Confirm with your home state and write it down

State rules vary, and this page describes the shape of the determination rather than any one state's version of it. Some states set their own requirements for advisers in the middle band, or have rules that interact with the federal line in ways this guide cannot cover. So confirm your conclusion with your home state's securities regulator, and with counsel if your figure sits anywhere near a line, before you file anything.

For illustration, the adviser, solo and serving working professionals within about a decade of retirement, works through this in order. The adviser projects regulatory assets under management from the clients expected to move, using the form's definition, and finds the figure well below the level that would require SEC registration, with room to spare before the buffer. The adviser reads the exceptions, sees that the clients all live in the home state and no other category fits, and confirms with the state's securities regulator that the state is where the firm registers. The adviser stays state-registered, and the rest of the guide follows the firm there.

You leave this step holding a written determination: the projected figure and how you calculated it, the regulator it points to, the exceptions you ruled out, and what the state regulator or counsel confirmed. It goes into the folder from the previous step, and the figure returns when you write the disclosure documents.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Have you calculated regulatory assets under management the way the [Form ADV](/glossary#form-adv) instructions define it, not the way you would describe your book?
- If you are projecting rather than measuring, how confident are you that the clients you are counting will move?
- Does your projected figure sit comfortably on one side of the line, or close enough that a good or bad year could move it across?
- Do you serve, or plan to serve, clients in enough states that the [multi-state exception](/glossary#multi-state-exception) could apply?
- Have you confirmed the determination with your state regulator, or with counsel, before treating it as settled?

## Common mistakes

- Using a marketing or account-statement figure instead of the defined regulatory one, and getting a different answer than the form will produce.
- Assuming the SEC is the more prestigious regulator and trying to file there, when the rules bar an adviser below the line from registering there.
- Ignoring the middle band and the home-state rules that govern it, then finding that the state and the SEC each expect the other to handle the firm.
- Treating the determination as done once. Assets grow, clients move, and the answer can change at an annual update.

## Which practice areas apply

- [Compliance and Supervision](/domains/compliance-and-supervision): The regulator you register with determines whose rules your compliance program must satisfy and who will examine it.
- [Records and Documentation](/domains/records-and-documentation): The calculation and written determination produced here are records you will be asked to show, and to reproduce, at every annual update.
- [Firm Economics](/domains/firm-economics): Growth in assets under management is what most often moves a firm across a line later, so the economic plan and the regulator are linked.
