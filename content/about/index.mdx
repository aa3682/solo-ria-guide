# About

The Independent Path is a free, public reference on establishing and running an independent registered investment advisory firm in the United States. This page covers what the guide is, where its material comes from, how it is kept current, and how you may reuse it.

## What this guide is — and what it is not

The guide follows the whole sequence of going independent, from the first question of whether the model fits you through the ongoing work of staying registered, and it covers the standing subjects — compliance, pricing, technology, firm economics — that keep running once the firm exists.

It is a general framework, not a plan for any specific firm. Requirements differ by state, by the shape of the practice, and by what the firm actually does for clients, so no single sequence is the right one for everybody. This is educational material: it explains how these firms are established and run. It is not legal, compliance, tax, or investment advice, it is not tailored to your situation, and reading it creates no professional relationship. Use it to learn the terrain and to ask sharper questions of the people you hire.

## How this guide is grounded

The project works under one constraint. The guide is written from public primary material rather than from the author's own registration history, because the author's background is industry experience rather than having personally registered a firm. The consequence is that every procedural claim has to trace to a document you can open yourself.

What counts as a primary source here is narrow and specific: statutes; rules and regulations as published by the agency that issued them; agency publications and guidance; the uniform regulatory forms and their official instructions; and what state securities regulators and their national association publish about registration, fees, and qualification. Commercial material is not a source for this guide — no vendor documentation, no consultant marketing, no paid coursework — and no commercial product is named or linked anywhere on the site.

The guide also follows a single default reader, an adviser registering with a state rather than with the SEC, and where the SEC-registered path diverges the page says so at the point of divergence rather than leaving you to find out later. For the reader-facing version of the same point, see the [Introduction](/introduction).

## How this guide is sourced

Every page is original writing. Nothing is lifted from a statute, a regulation, a training course, or commercial material, and nothing is reworded closely enough to be a paraphrase of someone else's sentences. Where a fact needs support — a requirement, a filing deadline, a document that has to be delivered — the page links the public source that establishes it and names the agency or body behind it. Worked examples use invented people and round numbers.

## How this guide is maintained

Pages never print a number set by law, regulation, or an agency. Fees, thresholds, limits, bond amounts, and deadlines all change, and a number typed into the middle of a paragraph is the thing most likely to go stale without anyone noticing. So the pages name the concept in plain language and link to [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures), which holds every such number with its year and its source. That page is reviewed once a year, which makes updating the guide one job on one page instead of a hunt across the site.
