# Glossary

This page defines the terms the rest of the guide uses, in plain language and in alphabetical order. Definitions say what a term means; any figure a term turns on — a limit, a threshold, a fee, a deadline — lives on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures) instead.

## A [#a]

### Advertisement [#advertisement]

For an investment adviser, broadly any communication that offers the firm's advisory services to people who are not already clients. The definition turns on who a communication reaches and what it offers, not on whether anyone paid to place it, so a website, a monthly letter, a talk at a community event, or a social post can each fall inside it. A piece written purely to explain something is still an advertisement if it reaches people who are not clients and holds the firm out as an adviser.

### Advisers Act [#advisers-act]

Short for the Investment Advisers Act, the federal statute that defines who is an investment adviser, sets the duties advisers owe their clients, and divides registration between the SEC and the states. Rules the SEC writes under it govern an SEC-registered adviser directly. A state-registered adviser is reached by parts of it too, and by state law that is usually modeled on it, so the Act sets the shape of the subject even where it is not the rulebook that binds the firm.

### Annual updating amendment [#annual-updating-amendment]

The yearly filing in which a registered firm confirms or corrects every answer on its registration form, timed to the firm's fiscal year end. It is the anchor event of the compliance calendar: what the firm told its regulator has to still be true, or be fixed, once a year. Items that cannot wait for it are corrected in an other-than-annual amendment instead. The deadline is set by rule and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#write-the-disclosure-documents).

## B [#b]

### Brochure supplement [#brochure-supplement]

The short document describing the individual who will actually give a client advice — that person's education and business background, any disciplinary history, other business activities, outside compensation, and who supervises the advice. It is Part 2B of Form ADV, and one is prepared for each person who advises. In a solo firm it describes the owner, which makes the supervision answer the part that takes thought. When it has to reach a client is set by rule and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#write-the-disclosure-documents).

### Broker-dealer [#broker-dealer]

A firm in the business of buying and selling securities, for customers or for its own account, registered and regulated under a different part of the securities laws than an investment adviser. Many readers of this guide are leaving one. The distinction matters because a broker-dealer's obligations, supervision structure, and compensation model differ from an adviser's, and because a broker-dealer is one of the institutions that can serve as a qualified custodian.

### Buffer on either side of the SEC line [#buffer-on-either-side-of-the-sec-line]

The allowance that lets a firm's assets drift modestly across the line separating state registration from SEC registration without forcing an immediate change of regulator. It exists so that ordinary growth or a market move does not push a firm back and forth between rulebooks. The level at which SEC registration becomes required, and the lower level below which an SEC-registered firm need not withdraw, are set by rule and are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#decide-if-independence-fits).

### Business continuity and succession plan [#business-continuity-and-succession-plan]

The written plan covering two things: how the firm keeps running through a shorter disruption, such as a lost office or a failed system, and who is authorized to step in and act for clients if the owner is unreachable, incapacitated, or unable to work. It is one of the policy documents the firm adopts before it can be approved. In a firm of one the succession half is the part most often skipped and the one clients and regulators most need answered, which means naming a specific person and putting the arrangement in writing rather than leaving it as an intention.

## C [#c]

### Chief compliance officer [#chief-compliance-officer]

The person a firm names as responsible for administering its compliance program. An SEC-registered adviser has to designate one by name and title; a state-registered adviser's obligation to name someone comes from state rule instead, and some states do not require the title at all for a firm with one owner. In a solo firm the honest answer is that the owner holds the role, and the compliance manual should say so plainly rather than describing an oversight structure the firm does not have.

### Claims-made policy [#claims-made-policy]

An insurance policy that responds to claims reported while the policy is in force, rather than to work performed while it was in force. Most errors and omissions coverage is written this way, which makes two details decisive: the retroactive date, and what happens to your past work once you stop paying premiums. A policy carried for years covers nothing after it lapses unless reporting after the end of the policy has been arranged separately.

### Code of ethics [#code-of-ethics]

The written standard of conduct a firm adopts for itself, covering in particular the personal securities transactions of the people who work there. It typically says what you must do before trading in a security you also recommend to clients, how you report your own holdings and trades, and how you handle gifts and outside business activity that could color your advice. Write rules a firm of one can actually follow: a pre-clearance step that depends on checking with someone else does not work when no one else exists.

### Compliance program review [#compliance-program-review]

The periodic, substantive review asking whether the firm's compliance program is still doing its job — whether the written procedures match what the firm actually does, and whether they still address the risks a changing practice faces. It is a separate obligation from the annual updating amendment, which asks only whether the registration form is accurate, and confusing the two is the most common way the review never happens. It should leave a dated record of what was checked, what was found, and what changed as a result. How often it is required, and what documentation goes with it, is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#compliance-and-supervision).

### Conflict of interest [#conflict-of-interest]

A circumstance in which the firm's own interest, or the interest of someone connected to it, could pull against a client's. Under an adviser's fiduciary duty a conflict has to be eliminated or disclosed fully enough that the client can give informed consent to it, which is why identifying conflicts is a standing exercise rather than a one-time list. It is not a judgment about anyone's character; it is a structural fact about how the firm is paid, who else it does business with, and what it recommends. A conflict the firm has decided is harmless is still a conflict, and the test is whether a client would want to know, not whether the adviser believes it changes the advice.

### CRD [#crd]

The central registration system FINRA operates, which holds an individual's registration and disclosure record across both broker-dealer and adviser registrations. A new individual filing attaches to the record already there, which is why the disclosure answers have to be read against what was filed before rather than answered from memory. It is closely linked to IARD, the system that carries the firm's own registration filings, and the same operator runs both.

### Custody [#custody]

A defined term meaning the firm holds, or can reach, client funds or securities — and it reaches further than holding assets outright. Having the authority to deduct your own fees from a client's account is generally custody, and so, in some circumstances, is holding a power of attorney broad enough to move money. Whether a given arrangement counts, and what it then requires, depends on the rule your regulator administers; the notice deadlines, statement frequencies, and verification triggers are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#set-up-custody-and-technology).

## D [#d]

### Deficiency letter [#deficiency-letter]

A regulator's written response to a registration filing identifying what has to be fixed before the application can proceed — a missing exhibit, a question about an inconsistent answer, a section of the brochure to revise. It is an ordinary part of review rather than a denial, and most applications draw at least a comment. What makes it expensive is the response deadline: an application left past the time the regulator allows can be treated as abandoned, which starts the filing over rather than merely delaying it.

### Discretionary authority [#discretionary-authority]

The authority to place trades or rebalance a client's account without asking the client each time, inside limits agreed in advance. The alternative, non-discretionary management, means the client approves each action before it happens. The choice shapes your daily workload, how quickly the firm can respond to a client's changing situation, and what the compliance program has to supervise, and in several states it is one of the arrangements that triggers a minimum net worth requirement or a surety bond.

### Disqualified person [#disqualified-person]

Someone whose disciplinary history bars a firm from paying them for a testimonial or an endorsement at all. Both the federal marketing rule and the state model rules written toward it work this way: certain past events put a person outside what the firm may compensate, whatever disclosure it would otherwise make. How far back the firm has to look, and which events count, is set by rule and by state and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#marketing-and-growth).

## E [#e]

### Endorsement [#endorsement]

A statement by someone who is not a client that recommends the firm or describes their experience of it. Endorsements, testimonials, and paid introductions are treated as one subject, because from a regulator's seat each is somebody other than the firm telling the public the firm is worth hiring. Whether the speaker received anything of value, and what conflict that creates, has to be disclosed where a reader will actually see it. The conditions attached to compensated arrangements are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#marketing-and-growth).

### Entitlement [#entitlement]

Authorized access to the electronic system advisers file through: an account, a named person at the firm permitted to log in and submit filings, and verification of that person's identity. Nothing can be submitted until it exists, and it runs on the system operator's timeline rather than yours. Requesting it early keeps it off the critical path, since no other part of the filing depends on it.

### Errors and omissions coverage [#errors-and-omissions-coverage]

Professional liability insurance for advice — the policy most independent advisory firms carry, responding to a claim that the firm's work caused a client a loss. What decides whether it helps is rarely the headline limit but the edges: the retroactive date, whether defense costs come out of the limit or sit outside it, and which activities are excluded. Data-breach exposure is often not carried by such a policy at all and is usually a separate line with its own terms.

### Examination [#examination]

A regulator's inspection of a registered firm — its books, its filings, its policies, and whether its practice matches what its documents say. It is not the same thing as the qualifying examination an individual passes in order to register, though this guide uses the word for both and the surrounding sentence settles which is meant. What most changes how an examination goes for a solo firm is whether the compliance work left a record at the time it happened.

## F [#f]

### Fiduciary duty [#fiduciary-duty]

The duty an investment adviser owes each client to act in that client's best interest, made up of a duty of care and a duty of loyalty. It is not satisfied by disclosure alone, although disclosure is how a conflict that cannot be removed is handled. It does not dictate how a firm charges — a percentage of assets, a flat retainer, an hourly rate, and a subscription are each compatible with it — but it does require the firm to name what its own fee structure rewards.

### FINRA [#finra]

The Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, the self-regulatory organization that oversees broker-dealers and the people registered with them. It does not regulate investment advisers. Advisers meet it mainly as the operator of the electronic filing and registration systems that the state and federal regulators use, and as the body that administers the qualifying examinations.

### Firm brochure [#firm-brochure]

The narrative document telling a prospective client what the firm does, what it charges, and where the firm's interests and the client's could pull apart. It is Part 2A of Form ADV: the form sets the topics it must cover and their order, but everything said under them is the firm's own writing. It is also the firm's controlling account of itself, which is why nothing the firm publishes elsewhere can promise more than the brochure describes. When it has to be delivered and updated is set by rule and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#write-the-disclosure-documents).

### Form ADV [#form-adv]

The uniform registration form an investment adviser files, in several parts: a structured part answering the regulator's questions about the firm, the narrative firm brochure and brochure supplement that clients receive, and a state-only part. It is where the firm's formal description of itself lives, which is what the annual updating amendment keeps current. A reviewer reads it against the brochure and against the individual's own filing, and disagreement between any two of them is what draws a comment.

### Form ADV Part 1B [#form-adv-part-1b]

The part of the registration form that only a state-registered adviser completes, answering the state's own questions about the firm on subjects such as bonding, custody arrangements, and financial condition. No client receives it. Its answers have to agree with the firm brochure, which is why it is drafted alongside the brochure rather than after it. An adviser on the SEC path does not complete it at all.

### Form CRS [#form-crs]

A short, standardized client relationship summary that an SEC-registered adviser prepares for retail clients, sitting alongside the firm brochure rather than replacing it. A state-registered adviser does not prepare one. It has to be amended and redelivered to existing clients whenever anything in it becomes materially inaccurate. Its page limit and its delivery deadlines are set by rule and are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#write-the-disclosure-documents).

### Form U4 [#form-u4]

The uniform form an individual files to register as an investment adviser representative, separate from the firm's own registration and usually submitted alongside it. It asks for employment history, other business activities, where you will work, and a set of yes-or-no disclosure questions about criminal, regulatory, civil, customer-complaint, and financial history. A yes answer does not by itself bar registration, because the regulator weighs what happened and when; an inaccurate answer is different, since the filing is signed as true.

### Form U5 [#form-u5]

The uniform form a firm files to document that an individual's association with it has ended, including any disclosure about how the relationship ended. It goes onto the departing person's own regulatory record, visible to regulators and, in redacted form, to the public. The prior firm controls what it says, so read it once it posts and know how to dispute it if it misstates what happened. How long the firm has to file it is set by rule and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#leave-and-transition-clients).

## H [#h]

### Hypothetical performance [#hypothetical-performance]

Investment results that were modeled, back-tested, or otherwise not earned in real client accounts. Presenting it is permitted in some form on both the state and SEC paths, but only with specific safeguards, because an illustration or a projection is not a track record and cannot be dressed as one. What a firm has to do before publishing such figures is set by rule and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#marketing-and-growth).

## I [#i]

### IARD [#iard]

The electronic system through which investment advisers — state-registered and SEC-registered alike — submit registration filings, amendments, and renewals, operated by FINRA on behalf of the regulators that use it. Entitled access to it is a prerequisite to filing anything, and the fees the operator charges are separate from whatever the regulator charges. It is also where the yearly renewal notice arrives.

### Independent verification [#independent-verification]

A check of client assets carried out by an independent accountant rather than by the firm, at a time the accountant chooses rather than one the firm schedules. It is one of the heavier obligations that custody can trigger, which is why the conditions keeping a fee-deducting adviser outside it are worth reading closely. What triggers it, and on what terms, is set by rule and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#set-up-custody-and-technology).

### Investment adviser representative [#investment-adviser-representative]

The individual who actually gives advice on the firm's behalf, registered separately from the firm and with requirements of their own: a qualifying examination or an accepted professional designation, an individual filing, often fingerprints, and in most states continuing education every year. In a solo firm you are both the firm's owner and its only representative, so both registrations have to be in order before the firm can do business. Approval of one is not approval of the other.

## M [#m]

### Mid-sized adviser band [#mid-sized-adviser-band]

The range of regulatory assets under management between the ceiling below which an adviser is generally barred from registering with the SEC and registers with its home state instead, and the threshold at or above which the SEC is its regulator. An adviser inside the band registers with its home state where that state requires registration and examines advisers, and with the SEC where it does not — which makes the home state, rather than anything about the firm, the deciding fact. All three amounts are set by statute and are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#decide-if-independence-fits).

### Minimum net worth [#minimum-net-worth]

An amount of net worth several states require a state-registered adviser to hold at all times, usually keyed to whether the firm has custody, holds discretionary authority, or accepts fees substantially in advance. It is a condition of registration rather than a business target, and falling below it is generally a reportable event on a short deadline, not something to fix quietly first. The amounts, the triggers, and the reporting deadline vary by state and are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#risk-and-insurance).

### Model rule [#model-rule]

A rule NASAA publishes as a template for the states, so that state requirements converge rather than being written from scratch in every jurisdiction. A model rule is not law and binds nobody until a state adopts it, in its own wording and on its own schedule — or declines to. Two state-registered advisers can therefore be operating under materially different rules in the same year, which is why this guide names the model and then sends you to your own regulator for what actually applies to you.

### Multi-state exception [#multi-state-exception]

A rule allowing an adviser that would otherwise have to register in a large number of states at once to register with the SEC instead, whatever assets it manages. It is the exception most likely to reach a solo firm whose clients are spread across the country. It also has to keep being true: if it stops applying, the firm returns to state registration on the timeline the rules set. The number of states that triggers it is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#choose-your-regulator).

## N [#n]

### NASAA [#nasaa]

The North American Securities Administrators Association, the membership organization of state and provincial securities regulators. It is not itself a regulator and cannot bind anyone. What it does is publish model rules, sponsor the qualifying examinations, and collect the state regulators' resources in one place, which is why most of what a state-registered adviser has to do began as a NASAA model that the reader's own state then adopted, adapted, or ignored.

### Notice filing [#notice-filing]

A filing an SEC-registered adviser makes with a state in place of registering there, usually with a fee attached. It exists because a state cannot register an adviser the SEC registers but can still require notice, and payment, from a firm doing business with its residents. A state-registered adviser makes no notice filing in its home state. How much business in a state triggers one, and what the state charges, varies by state and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#file-and-get-approved).

## O [#o]

### Other-than-annual amendment [#other-than-annual-amendment]

An amendment to the registration form filed promptly when a particular answer stops being accurate, rather than waiting for the next annual cycle. The form's own instructions identify which items work this way; disciplinary and custody answers are most often among them. The habit that keeps this manageable is asking, every time something about the firm changes, whether it touches one of those items.

## P [#p]

### Plain English [#plain-english]

As a disclosure standard, the requirement that the firm brochure and the brochure supplement be written in ordinary language, in the firm's own voice rather than the language of a filing, and calibrated to the financial sophistication of the clients who will read them. It is a result rather than a box to check: a document can satisfy every content requirement and still leave a client unclear about what they are buying. This guide also uses the phrase in its everyday sense, meaning writing without jargon; the standard defined here is the narrower one the disclosure instructions impose.

### Professional designation [#professional-designation]

A credential a state may accept in place of the qualifying examination when it registers an investment adviser representative. Acceptance is decided state by state and some states accept none, so a designation is never a waiver everywhere. Which designations a state accepts, if any, is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#qualify-as-an-adviser).

## Q [#q]

### Qualified custodian [#qualified-custodian]

An institution that meets the regulatory definition of a custodian — a bank, a registered broker-dealer, or another qualifying institution — with which client funds and securities generally have to be held, rather than in an account the adviser controls directly. Choosing one means confirming it can actually hold and trade the account types and instruments the practice plans to offer, and that it will send account statements directly to clients rather than through the adviser. Its onboarding takes real time and belongs on the critical path well before the first client conversation.

### Qualifying examination [#qualifying-examination]

The examination an individual passes to demonstrate competence before registering as an investment adviser representative. The examinations are written under NASAA's sponsorship and administered by FINRA, and which one, or which combination of them, a state accepts is set by state rule. A passed examination generally stays valid while you remain registered in a role that relies on it, and lapses after a gap without one. The identifiers, the lapse period, and the designations accepted instead are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#qualify-as-an-adviser).

## R [#r]

### Recordkeeping scheme [#recordkeeping]

The firm's written plan for every record it is required to keep: what format each category lives in, where it is stored, who can reach it, and how it is backed up. It is written once, before the firm is approved, and then has to keep describing a firm that never stops generating new kinds of records. A scheme built around keeping everything, without deciding where any specific record actually lives, fails the only test that matters — whether you could produce a particular record when someone asks for it.

### Recruiting protocol [#recruiting-protocol]

An arrangement some firms join that lets a departing representative take specific, limited client information without triggering a claim under agreements they would otherwise be bound by. Whether your current firm belongs to one, and what it actually permits, is a fact to confirm directly rather than assume; a firm outside one leaves you bound by your own agreement with none of a protocol's carve-outs available. Even where one applies it typically covers a narrow slice of information and leaves the rest governed by whatever you signed.

### Registered investment adviser [#registered-investment-adviser]

A firm in the business of advising others about securities for compensation that has registered as an investment adviser, either with a state securities regulator or with the SEC. Until that registration is effective the firm cannot hold itself out as one, open an account for a client, or charge an advisory fee. Registration is of the firm; the person who actually gives the advice registers separately, as an investment adviser representative.

### Regulatory assets under management [#regulatory-assets-under-management]

The defined figure that decides which regulator registers a firm: the securities portfolios the firm continuously and regularly supervises or manages, calculated the way the registration form's instructions describe. It can differ from the number you would use to describe your book, because the definition is not intuitive, and a firm that does not exist yet projects it rather than measures it. The lines it is compared against are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#decide-if-independence-fits).

### Renewal window [#renewal-window]

The period each year in which a firm has to complete the renewal of its registration, after the regulator issues a renewal notice through the electronic filing system. Renewal is separate from keeping the registration form accurate: a firm can file every amendment correctly and still let the registration lapse by missing the window. The window and the renewal fee are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#stay-registered-and-compliant).

### Restrictive covenant [#restrictive-covenant]

A term in an employment agreement limiting what you may do after you leave — most often a non-solicitation clause barring you from approaching the firm's clients, or a non-compete clause barring you from working in the same business nearby. What any of them actually binds you to depends on your own agreement and your state's law, which differ enough that a colleague's experience elsewhere tells you nothing about your own. Read them, with counsel, before contacting a single client.

### Retention period [#retention-period]

How long the firm has to preserve each category of record before it may be discarded. The periods differ by category, and by whether the firm is state-registered or SEC-registered; they are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#write-the-policies). Treat a retention period as a floor rather than a plan, since a record tied to a dispute, a departed client, or an unusual transaction is often worth keeping well past the date the rule allows you to drop it.

### Retroactive date [#retroactive-date]

The date on a claims-made insurance policy that fixes how far back into your past work the policy reaches. Work done before it is not covered, however long you have carried the policy since. Along with what happens when you stop carrying coverage, it is the detail that decides whether errors and omissions coverage actually helps when a claim arrives, which makes it worth confirming at each renewal rather than once at purchase.

## S [#s]

### SEC-registered adviser [#sec-registered-adviser]

A firm whose regulator is the SEC rather than a state securities regulator, generally because its regulatory assets under management sit at or above the threshold, or because an exception put it there. This is not the guide's default scenario; where the SEC path diverges, the page in question marks the divergence. An SEC-registered adviser still makes notice filings in the states where it does enough business, and the individual giving advice may still register with the state where they have a place of business.

### Securities and Exchange Commission [#securities-and-exchange-commission]

The federal agency that administers the securities laws, writes the rules under the Advisers Act, and registers and examines the advisers above the line separating federal from state registration. Commonly shortened to the SEC. Most new solo firms never register with it, but its rules still set much of the shape of this subject, because state requirements are frequently modeled on them.

### Standardized account transfer [#standardized-account-transfer]

The process most custodians and broker-dealers participate in for moving a client's account from one institution to another: a transfer request identifies the account and the receiving custodian, the two institutions exchange it electronically, and the assets move without the client selling out of positions and repurchasing them. Not everything moves this way — some account types, proprietary investments, and unusual holdings need extra paperwork or cannot move across in their current form — so flag those cases to the client before the request goes in. The windows each side has to move a transfer along are set by rule and are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#leave-and-transition-clients).

### State-registered adviser [#state-registered-adviser]

A firm whose regulator is the securities regulator of its home state rather than the SEC. This is where almost every new solo practice starts, and it is the scenario this guide follows by default. A state-registered adviser completes the state-only part of the registration form, meets its state's rules on custody, bonding, net worth, and recordkeeping, and renews with the state each year — and because those rules are set state by state, the guide names each concept and sends you to your own regulator for the version that binds you.

### Supervision [#supervision]

The firm's obligation to oversee the advice it gives and the conduct of the people who give it. The plain meaning — someone senior reviewing someone junior's work — does not describe a firm of one, but the obligation applies anyway; the solo version replaces a second person with structure, such as a written log of your own trades checked on a fixed schedule and a periodic sample of your own client files read the way an examiner would read them. If the firm ever adds anyone whose work touches client accounts, supervision becomes oversight of another person's work, and the compliance manual has to say so.

### Surety bond [#surety-bond]

A bond several states require a state-registered adviser to post, usually keyed to whether the firm has custody, holds discretionary authority, or accepts fees substantially in advance. It is not insurance for the firm: it is a promise to a third party, backed by an insurer, that pays a client if the firm cannot — and the surety expects the firm to repay it afterward. Whether a bond is required at all, and in what amount, varies by state and is on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#risk-and-insurance).

## T [#t]

### Testimonial [#testimonial]

A statement by a current or former client about their experience of the firm. Testimonials, endorsements, and paid introductions are treated as one subject, and using any of them carries a disclosure obligation and a paper trail: whether the speaker is a client, whether they received anything of value, and what conflict that creates all have to be disclosed where a reader will see them. Whether a state-registered adviser may use testimonials at all depends on whether the state has adopted the amended model rules; the conditions attached are on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#marketing-and-growth).

## U [#u]

### Uniform Securities Act [#uniform-securities-act]

The model state securities statute that most states' own securities laws are built on. More than one version of it exists, and the states are split between an older version and a later revision, which is why a NASAA model rule is often published twice — once in the wording each version calls for. When a rule refers to jurisdictions on one act or the other, that split is what it means.
